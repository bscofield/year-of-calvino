Hermit in Paris: Autobiographical Writings

Eremita a Parigi

  • Original Publication: 1994
  • Translator: Martin McLaughlin (2003)
  • Read 21 November

Calvino wasn't a big fan of writing his own biography, and I think I've come to the conclusion that I'm not a big fan of reading his biographies -- at least, not in comparison to his fiction. This was fine, but I'm not particularly interested in his political evolution (at least right now; maybe with a deeper grounding in the political history of Europe in general and Italy in particular post-WW2 I'd find it more engaging. Be that as it may). Really, the only part that stood out to me was seeing how he described similar events differently -- or similarly -- in different pieces, and I could've gotten that from a much shorter work.

His American travelogue was more interesting, but then that was closer to his non-fiction essays in previous collections than it was to autobiography, and I have a much closer relationship, and stronger grounding in the history, of what he wrote about there.
