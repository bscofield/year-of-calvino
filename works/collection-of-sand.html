Collection of Sand

Collezione di sabbia

  • Original Publication: 1984
  • Translator: Martin McLaughlin (2013)
  • Read 20 September

I found this much more accessible than The Uses of Literature; I suppose that's because Calvino was more descriptive of the works he was talking about, instead of assuming the readers' (deep) familiarity? Regardless of why, this was a much more readable collection, and I deeply enjoyed it.

"The Narrative of Trajan's Column" was a standout, and got me thinking about the translation of vision to idea and then to artifact, which itself is then perceived via vision, translated into Calvino's prose, and then read and transformed to idea once again by the reader. There's mediation and interpretation at every step, but the end result is something that -- if not the same thing that you'd get experiencing the original events yourself, or by seeing the column yourself -- is valuable and meaningful on its own.

I was a bit put off by the travel writing, especially about Japan. While the writing itself was lovely, there was something about Calvino's consistent focus on the detail that felt ... separating, and slightly othering. I could sense his appreciation for the cultures he was experiencing, but the depth of his attention made for at least a small barrier that I had trouble crossing (though, maybe the same would be true if he were writing about Italy or Paris? I imagine I'll have a better sense of that as I read more of his non-fiction in the coming months.)
